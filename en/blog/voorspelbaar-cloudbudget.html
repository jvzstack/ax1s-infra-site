5 steps to a predictable cloud budget

Tom Prager·18 Aug 2026·4 min read

Last year, plus a margin

Most cloud budgets aren't a budget. It's last year, plus a margin. By the end of the quarter it turns out nobody can explain exactly why it turned out differently. Not because something went wrong, but because there was never a structure in place to predict it.

These five steps are what we see recurring at organisations that do get their cloud budget under control.

1. Start with current usage

Many companies first set an amount and look for the justification afterwards. Rather, first look at what is actually running now, who uses it and why. At AX1S we find that this is the step that is skipped most often. Want a quick first indication without sitting down for it yourself? The FinOps Radar gives a first picture of your savings potential within 2 minutes.

2. Work with multiple scenarios

Normal usage, growth and peak load produce three completely different amounts. Anyone who calculates with just one fixed number is almost guaranteed to be off. This is particularly relevant for AI workloads: a pilot with 50 users behaves completely differently financially from the same feature with 5,000 users. With the AI Cost Calculator you can calculate that growth, including the breakdown and projection.

3. Define who is responsible for what

A predictable budget requires clear ownership: who may decide on new capacity, new tools, or new AI integrations. Without that ownership a familiar pattern emerges: engineering builds, finance pays, and nobody has a real overview. At AX1S we see this in almost every intake conversation: the question "who decided on this?" more often gets a shrug than a name.

4. Use automatic alerting, not monthly reports

A report at the end of the month comes too late; the money has already been spent. Set up interim alerting as soon as a team approaches a preset percentage of the budget, so you can adjust before it becomes an overrun.

5. Review the budget every quarter, not every year

Cloud and AI environments change faster than an annual budgeting cycle can keep up with. A budget set once a year is outdated again within a few months. Build in a fixed quarterly moment at which usage, scenarios and ownership are reviewed again. Still unsure whether your reserved instances or savings plans are the right starting point? The RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator compares on-demand, reserved instances and savings plans without you having to leave an email address.

A predictable cloud budget isn't a matter of guessing better, but of structure: usage as the starting point, scenarios instead of one number, clear ownership, interim alerting, and a fixed rhythm.

Can you right now, without looking, say what your cloud budget will do this quarter at 20% growth? If not, that is exactly where step 2 makes the difference.
