Nobody feels they own the cloud bill

Jelle van Zantwijk·23 Sep 2026·3 min read
Short answer

With a shared cloud bill nobody feels responsible, just like one energy bill for five housemates. The teams that cause costs never see the bill: it lands with finance, in a dashboard they do not read. This is not laziness but a structural problem. As long as costs and responsibility do not meet, saving depends on goodwill.

Why does a shared bill work so badly?

Five housemates, one energy bill. Nobody checks the thermostat, because it is “shared” anyway. That is how a cloud bill works in many companies too.

What does that look like in practice?

A developer forgets to switch off a test environment. A team picks the most expensive instance “to be safe”. None of them sees the bill. It lands with finance, in a dashboard they do not read.

Is this a behaviour problem?

No, it is not laziness but a structural problem. As long as the people who cause the costs do not see the bill, saving depends on goodwill. Goodwill does not scale and disappears as soon as a deadline appears.

How do you bring costs and responsibility together?

  • Show the teams that spin up resources their own costs (showback), not just finance.
  • Assign an owner to every workload. Resource tagging is the foundation for that.
  • Discuss costs where the decisions are made, in the team itself, not just in a monthly report for finance.

Frequently asked questions

Who is responsible for cloud costs in an organisation?

Responsibility belongs with whoever makes the decision: the teams that spin up resources. Finance and IT support them with insight and guardrails.

What is showback?

Showback shows teams what their resources cost, without charging those costs directly to their budget. It makes costs visible to whoever causes them.

Why does cloud waste persist when everyone wants to save?

Because the people who cause costs often do not see the bill. Without visibility and ownership, saving depends on goodwill rather than structure.
