Resource tagging: the foundation of FinOps

Tom Prager·5 Oct 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Resource tagging is the single most important technical foundation for FinOps, yet most organisations treat it as an afterthought. A minimal, enforced set of tags (owner, environment, service or product, and cost centre), attached at creation through Infrastructure as Code and cloud policies, can push taggable spend coverage above 90 percent and turn an opaque bill into actionable showback or chargeback.

Which tags do you need at minimum?

Start small. Four keys are enough to be able to allocate a bill:

  • Owner: who is responsible for this resource?
  • Environment: production, test or development.
  • Service or product: what does this resource belong to?
  • Cost centre: where are the costs ultimately booked?

How do you approach tagging in 2026?

The real shift in 2025 and 2026 is from “tag everything perfectly” to a pragmatic approach:

  • Freeze four keys.
  • Enforce them in non-production first.
  • Measure coverage weighted by spend, not by number of resources.
  • Retro-tag the expensive resources.

When does tagging become a lever instead of a burden?

When tagging is defined once, encoded in modules and policies, and audited monthly with a clear owner for untagged resources. Then it stops being a governance burden and becomes the lever that connects engineering decisions to budgets, optimisation and business value.

Frequently asked questions

What is resource tagging?

Resource tagging is attaching labels, such as owner, environment and cost centre, to cloud resources so that costs can be allocated to teams, products and budgets.

What is the difference between showback and chargeback?

Showback shows teams what their resources cost. With chargeback those costs are actually charged to the team’s budget.

How high should tag coverage be?

A minimal, enforced set of tags can push taggable spend coverage above 90 percent. Measure coverage weighted by spend so the expensive resources are tagged first.
