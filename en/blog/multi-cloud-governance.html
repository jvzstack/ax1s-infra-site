Multi-cloud governance without the complexity

Tom Prager·5 Jun 2026·7 min read

Why organisations spread across multiple clouds

Multi-cloud rarely arises from a deliberate strategy. More often it's the result of an acquisition, a team that independently chose a different provider, or a specific service that is simply better elsewhere. The problem is not the presence of multiple clouds as such. The problem is that governance rarely grows along with that reality.

The three most common governance gaps

  • Fragmented cost visibility: every provider has its own billing format and dashboards, so nobody has the overall picture.
  • Inconsistent tagging and naming policy: what is a "project" tag in one cloud is called "cost-center" elsewhere, resulting in duplicate work and errors.
  • Separate security and access models: every platform has its own IAM model, making auditing and compliance needlessly complex.

Governance without a dedicated platform team

You don't need a large internal platform team to solve this. Most of the gain lies in three relatively simple steps:

1. One shared cost model, regardless of the source

A cloud-agnostic cost report, in which usage from all providers is normalised to the same dimensions (team, project, environment), gives immediate overview without having to report separately per platform.

2. One tagging standard, centrally enforced

Define a minimal set of mandatory tags that mean the same on every platform, and enforce this with automated policy checks when resources are created. This prevents governance from depending on individual discipline.

3. Regular cross-cloud reviews

A recurring (for example monthly) overview in which usage, costs and deviations across all clouds are discussed prevents each platform from living its own isolated life.

Multi-cloud governance isn't about centralising everything. It's about consistent baseline agreements, while teams keep the freedom to choose the right platform per workload.

When to consider consolidating?

Sometimes the conclusion after a governance review is that consolidating to fewer providers does make sense, particularly when the operational overhead of multiple platforms no longer outweighs the benefits. That is, however, an outcome of good governance, not a prerequisite for it.
