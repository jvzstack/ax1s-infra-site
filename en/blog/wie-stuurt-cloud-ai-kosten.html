Who steers cloud and AI costs?

Tom Prager·19 Aug 2026·3 min read

Four questions that quickly expose ownership

At most organisations there is no doubt about who pays the invoices. There is, however, about who actually steers what is being spent.

These four questions expose that quickly. Ask them internally, and the answer (or the lack of one) immediately tells you where ownership is missing.

1. Who may decide to switch on new cloud or AI capacity?

At many organisations the answer is: anyone with the right access permissions. A developer can put a new instance or a new AI model live without a financial check in between. That is not a matter of carelessness. It was simply never defined who may make that decision, and who is responsible for the result afterwards.

2. Who sees a cost increase first, and how quickly?

At most companies the answer lies somewhere between "at the end of the month" and "if finance happens to notice." By then the money has already been spent, and adjusting is no longer possible, only explaining. Whoever steers sees a deviation within days, not at the end of a billing cycle.

3. Who can explain why a cost item has risen, without first having to find out?

At AX1S we often ask this first during an intake: "why did this item rise last month?" The answer is rarely immediate. Usually a search through invoices and dashboards comes first before an explanation follows. That delay is itself the answer: if nobody knows right away, nobody is actively steering on it either.

4. Who is held accountable when the budget is exceeded?

Not who bears it on the balance sheet, but who is in the conversation when it goes wrong. At many organisations that is nobody specific; the overrun is simply accepted as "just how it went." Without a point of accountability, cost management remains non-committal, however good the intentions.

Four short questions, and usually it becomes clear from the first one what is wrong. Not because of ill will, but because steering was never explicitly assigned.

Ask these four questions at your next team meeting. The silence that follows is often the clearest answer.
