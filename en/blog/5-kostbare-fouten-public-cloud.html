5 costly mistakes that make your public cloud needlessly expensive

Jelle van Zantwijk·17 Aug 2026·5 min read

It's rarely one wrong setting

It lies in architecture, capacity and contracts that no longer match how something is actually used. That makes these mistakes harder to find than an isolated misconfiguration, but they are the pattern we see most often at AX1S in cloud bills that come in far higher than necessary. These are the five that most often carry through into the bill.

1. You only calculate with today's usage

What happens to costs if the number of users, transactions or the amount of data doubles? Anyone who doesn't ask that question upfront is confronted with it at the first growth spurt. So calculate in advance with several scenarios: normal usage, growth and peak load, so the architecture works not just for today.

2. You reserve capacity "to be safe"

Unused capacity costs money too, even when nothing runs on it. So look at actual usage and, where possible, make sure capacity can scale up and down automatically, instead of sizing well above need by default.

3. You commit to long-term obligations too early

Reserved Instances and Savings Plans can deliver a lot of benefit, but only if you actually use the capacity. So only commit once usage is predictable enough. (Not sure which form suits you? Read our article on Reserved Instances vs. Savings Plans, or calculate it directly with our RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator.)

4. You only look at compute

A cloud environment consists of more than servers. Storage, databases, backups, networking, logging and managed services often weigh at least as heavily in the total bill. So look at the cost of the entire workload, not just its most visible component.

5. You only look at costs once the environment is already running

You can still optimise then, but important choices have often already been made. So include costs during the design phase. Not only: does this architecture work technically? But also: what does this architecture cost at growth and over the full period of use?
