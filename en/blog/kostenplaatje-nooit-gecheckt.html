Oh no, nobody ever checked this cost item

Jelle van Zantwijk·25 Aug 2026·3 min read

Two years ago a logical choice

Two years ago a reserved instance was purchased. At the time a logical choice: the team knew exactly what would be running, the discount was attractive, and the commitment seemed a sensible investment.

A lot has changed since. A migration to a new architecture, a team that was split up, a product that was developed further. Nobody ever checked whether that old commitment still fits what is actually running now.

Stable, until someone asks

The amount still sits neatly in the monthly report. Unchanged, quarter after quarter. To anyone glancing at it, that looks reassuring: stable, predictable, no surprises.

Until someone, during a scan, asks the question nobody had asked any more: what is this actually still running on? The answer takes longer than expected. The person who made the original choice no longer works there. The underlying documentation can't be found. And when usage is finally laid next to it, the commitment turns out to be utilised for only a fraction.

For two years, capacity has been paid for that largely went unused, not because anyone was negligent, but because nobody ever asked the question the scan now did.

That is the pattern behind most outdated cost items. Not one big mistake, but the simple fact that nobody looked at it any more once the amount felt "normal".
