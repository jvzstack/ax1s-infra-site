A FinOps tool is not FinOps

Jelle van Zantwijk·22 Sep 2026·3 min read
Short answer

A FinOps tool shows what you spend. FinOps is about what you do with it: who is responsible, which trade-off you make between speed and cost, and whether insight leads to action in the teams that spin up resources. Without that process, a tool is just another subscription, not a saving.

What is the difference between a FinOps tool and FinOps?

Many companies think they are doing FinOps as soon as a tool is running that maps out their costs. Charts, a few alerts, done. But a tool only shows what you spend. FinOps is about what you do with it.

Which questions does FinOps answer that a tool does not?

  • Who is responsible for which costs?
  • Which trade-off do you make between speed and cost?
  • Does insight also lead to action in the teams that spin up the resources?

Why does a tool without a process deliver nothing?

Without that process, the tool becomes an extra subscription, not a saving. We see a dashboard full of red flags that nobody acts on often enough. The insight is there, but nobody owns the action.

So where do you start?

At AX1S we start with who makes which decision. Only then do we look at tooling. How far your organisation has come is shown in the FinOps maturity model, and the FinOps Radar gives a first indication in two minutes.

Frequently asked questions

What is FinOps?

FinOps is the practice in which finance, IT and business jointly take responsibility for cloud and technology costs. It combines insight, optimisation and a structural process. See also What is FinOps?.

Do I need a FinOps tool?

Tooling supports FinOps but is not the starting point. First establish who makes which decision and who owns which costs. Then choose tooling that supports that process.

How do I know whether we do FinOps or only have a tool?

Check whether insight leads to action. Are there owners, agreements on trade-offs and teams that act on the numbers? Then you are doing FinOps. If you mostly see dashboards nobody acts on, you have a tool.
