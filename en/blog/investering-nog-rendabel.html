Wait, is this investment still profitable?

Tom Prager·26 Aug 2026·3 min read

Eighteen months ago, a clear business case

Eighteen months ago an AI model went live that analyses customer data to make personalised recommendations. The business case was clear: higher conversion, satisfied customers, an investment that would pay for itself within a year.

The model is still running. The recommendations work, customers respond positively, and nobody has had reason to doubt it. That is exactly the problem: because it functions well, nobody has ever revisited the original calculation.

What has changed in the meantime

A lot has changed in the meantime. The customer base has grown, which means the model is called more often than at the start. Provider prices have risen slightly. And the conversion improvement the model was supposed to deliver has not been measured separately since the first months after go-live.

When someone finally takes the plunge and redoes the calculation, the picture turns out different from expected. The costs, through growth and price rises combined, have almost doubled compared with the original estimate. The return has never been re-measured, so nobody can say with certainty whether it has grown along.

The model isn't necessarily a bad investment. But nobody can currently say out loud whether that is still true, and that is a different problem from a bad business case: it is a business case that has never been tested again.

That is a pattern that occurs more often than you'd think. An investment is approved based on a calculation at one point in time, and then keeps running indefinitely without anyone repeating that calculation.
