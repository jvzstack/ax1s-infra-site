Frequently asked questions about cloud costs and FinOps

Jelle van Zantwijk·6 Aug 2026·7 min read

These are the questions we get most often about cloud costs and FinOps, gathered in one place. No jargon contest, just direct answers. For the underlying terms you can also consult the FinOps Glossary.

Why cloud costs get out of hand

Why are our cloud costs rising faster than our revenue?

Cloud infrastructure scales with usage, but without active steering it also scales with carelessness: new features run on new resources, old resources are rarely cleaned up, and nobody feels the bill directly. Revenue growth and technology growth therefore often drift apart until someone explicitly steers on it.

How much of an average cloud budget is waste?

That differs greatly per organisation, but unused cloud capacity often quickly eats up 10 to 15 percent of the cloud budget as soon as nobody structurally tracks usage.

What are signs that an organisation has no grip on cloud costs?

Nobody can explain in one sentence what the cloud budget is spent on, the invoice is opened with surprise afterwards instead of being predicted beforehand, and nobody is explicitly responsible for the costs, only for the technology behind them.

Saving on cloud costs: the practical side

What is rightsizing and how much does it deliver?

Rightsizing is adjusting the capacity of a resource to actual usage. Instances are often chosen too generously at the start of a project and never reconsidered afterwards. Based on usage data, 20 to 40 percent savings can usually be achieved on the workload concerned, without any impact on performance.

What is the difference between Reserved Instances and Savings Plans?

Both are commitment discounts in exchange for a usage or spend commitment. A Reserved Instance is tied to a specific instance type and region, a Savings Plan commits to a fixed amount of compute spend per hour, regardless of which instance type you use. Savings Plans are more flexible, Reserved Instances sometimes give a slightly higher discount for highly predictable workloads. (The full comparison is in Reserved Instances vs. Savings Plans.)

How do you make cloud costs visible per team or customer?

Through cost allocation: consistent tagging on team, project and environment, followed by chargeback or showback so teams see for themselves what their infrastructure costs. Once that is visible, behaviour often changes by itself.

What about the costs of AI and LLMs within FinOps?

AI cost management has become a focus area of its own within FinOps: compute for training and inference, API costs from model providers and data storage quickly add up and are often not yet separately visible in existing cost reports. The same discipline (measure, allocate, optimise) applies, only the tooling and the recognition of waste are still less mature than with traditional cloud compute.

FinOps as a practice

What exactly is FinOps?

In short: teams gain visibility into their own cloud and AI usage and take responsibility for it, instead of finance checking it afterwards. That cross-functional interplay between finance, engineering and operations is the core of FinOps. (See also What is FinOps? for the full explanation.)

What is the FinOps Lifecycle?

The FinOps Lifecycle consists of three continuous phases: Inform (visibility and cost allocation), Optimize (rightsizing, commitments and eliminating waste) and Operate (embedding in processes and team behaviour). The phases are repeated, not gone through once.

Who within an organisation is responsible for cloud costs?

In practice nobody, unless it is explicitly assigned. Engineering mainly optimises for performance and speed, finance sees the bill but often lacks technical context. FinOps is meant to make those two worlds speak the same language, with a clear owner for costs per team.

Is FinOps only relevant for large companies?

No. Scale-ups and SaaS companies feel the impact of unmanaged cloud costs sooner, because infrastructure costs have a more direct effect on margin and cash runway. The sooner the basics are in place (visibility, tagging, ownership), the less needs cleaning up later.

Outsourcing FinOps or doing it yourself

Can you implement FinOps yourself or do you need an external party?

Both are possible. Many organisations start with the basics themselves, tagging and a first cost overview, and bring in an external party once it goes beyond clean-up: setting up a structural framework, negotiating commitments, or creating ownership between teams that never prioritise it themselves.

How much can I save with a FinOps engagement?

That depends on the starting point: the more waste and the less insight there is now, the greater the potential. Customers of AX1S Infra achieve an average saving of 23% on their cloud and compute spend after a focused engagement. (Read the full case.)

How long does it take before you see results?

This differs per organisation and engagement, but in practice we often see quick wins after the first few weeks, such as cleaning up unused resources, followed by structural optimisation that runs for months.
