Cloud economics is becoming compute economics

Jelle van Zantwijk·3 Oct 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Cloud economics used to be about usage: consume more, pay more. AI workloads depend on expensive GPUs, reserved capacity and long-term infrastructure commitments. Companies are therefore no longer only paying for what they use, but also committing to compute capacity in advance. The FinOps question shifts from “what are we spending?” to “what compute are we committing to, and are we using it efficiently?”

What does AI change about cloud costs?

For years, cloud economics was mainly about usage. Consume more, pay more. AI changes that. AI workloads increasingly depend on expensive GPUs, reserved capacity and long-term infrastructure commitments.

Why do commitments become more important?

Companies are no longer only paying for what they use. They also commit to compute up front. That means unused capacity costs as much as used capacity, and the risk of waste moves from individual resources to the size and duration of the commitment. For how commitment discounts work, read Reserved Instances vs. Savings Plans.

What does the market say?

Reuters reported on 29 September 2026 that Anthropic has committed at least $518B to AI infrastructure over the next decade. That is an extreme example, but it shows the direction: capacity is committed in advance rather than only paid for afterwards.

Which questions now belong to FinOps?

  • What compute capacity are we committed to, and for how long?
  • How efficiently are we using that capacity?
  • Which workloads justify a commitment, and which do not?

Frequently asked questions

What is compute economics?

Compute economics is the shift from paying for usage to committing to compute in advance, such as GPUs and reserved capacity. The core question is not only what you spend, but what you commit to.

Why does AI cost management differ from classic cloud cost management?

With AI, costs are more often tied to expensive GPUs and long-term capacity commitments. You therefore also pay for capacity you are not yet, or not fully, using.

What is a compute commitment?

An agreement to buy a certain amount of compute capacity in advance, usually for one or more years, often in exchange for a lower price or guaranteed availability.
