3 steps to find waste in the cloud

Jelle van Zantwijk·20 Aug 2026·4 min read

A pile-up of small choices

Waste in the cloud is rarely the result of one big mistake. It builds up from small choices that seemed logical at the time.

These three steps map it out systematically, without you having to set up a full FinOps engagement right away.

1. Compare allocated capacity with actual usage

Most waste isn't in what is running, but in what has been set up needlessly large. Servers, storage and databases are often configured more generously than needed, "to be safe," and then stay unchanged for years. Put allocated capacity next to actual usage over the past 30 days. Anything with structurally low utilisation is a first candidate to downsize or switch off.

2. Look for resources without an owner

Over time every cloud environment collects resources that were once created for a specific project, which has since been completed or stopped. The resource itself happily keeps running, and nobody feels responsible for it any more. Search the environment by tags, labels and creation date. Anything without a clear owner or an actively linked project deserves a conversation: still needed, or simply never switched off. At AX1S this comes up in almost every scan, often accounting for a considerable part of the first savings found. With the FinOps Radar you get a first indication within 2 minutes of how much of this there could be at your organisation.

3. Check whether contracts still match current usage

Reserved instances and savings plans were once taken out based on an expected usage pattern. If that pattern has changed, for example through a migration or an architecture change, the contract often stays in place while no longer being optimal. Compare current commitments with actual usage via the RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator. A contract that was once advantageous may by now be costing money instead of saving it.

Finding waste is rarely complicated. It mainly takes looking for it, something that quickly gets skipped in the daily rush.

When did someone last take a critical look at everything that is running, instead of at the invoices coming in?
