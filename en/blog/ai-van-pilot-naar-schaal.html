Scaling AI beyond the pilot: the real business case

Jelle van Zantwijk·7 Oct 2026·3 min read
Short answer

Many AI projects show value, but scaling is the real problem. According to research reported by Reuters, nearly 75% of companies see positive financial results from AI projects, but fewer than a third manage to get them beyond the pilot phase. Once a pilot goes into production, the costs change and AI becomes a cost, governance and scalability question.

Does AI work? That is not the only question

Research reported by Reuters on 1 October 2026 shows that nearly 75% of companies see positive financial results from AI projects. But fewer than a third manage to get those projects beyond the pilot phase.

So the question is not only “does AI work?”, but above all: “can we also make it scalable and financially manageable?”

Which costs change once a pilot goes into production?

  • More model and API usage
  • More cloud infrastructure
  • Integration with existing systems
  • Security and governance
  • Structural operating costs

What does this mean for Finance and IT?

AI is not only an innovation question. It becomes a cost, governance and scalability question. The real business case therefore often starts only after the successful pilot.

Before scaling up, calculate what AI costs at volume, for example with the AI cost calculator. For more on how pilots get out of hand, read This AI project looked cheap, until the bill arrived.

Source: Reuters, 1 October 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What does the research reported by Reuters say?

Nearly 75% of companies see positive financial results from AI projects, but fewer than a third get those projects beyond the pilot phase.

Which costs are added when an AI pilot goes into production?

More model and API usage, more cloud infrastructure, integration with existing systems, security and governance, and structural operating costs.

How do you make AI financially scalable?

Before scaling up, calculate what it costs per user or transaction at volume, establish ownership of the costs and track usage from day one.
