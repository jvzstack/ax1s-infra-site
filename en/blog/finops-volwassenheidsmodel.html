The FinOps maturity model: where does your organisation stand?

Jelle van Zantwijk·19 Jun 2026·5 min read

Crawl, walk, run

FinOps is not a project with an end date, but a growth path. The FinOps Foundation describes this path in three phases: Crawl, Walk and Run, where each phase represents a deeper level of cost awareness, automation and collaboration between engineering, finance and business. Most organisations underestimate which phase they are actually in, and that is exactly where savings opportunities remain.

Crawl: visibility as the starting point

In this phase cost data is fragmented, consistent tagging is missing and cost control is reactive, usually only when the invoice arrives. Recognisable by loose spreadsheets, a single dashboard nobody consults structurally, and surprise afterwards at spikes in usage. This is nothing to be ashamed of: almost every organisation starts here.

Walk: from reactive to proactive

Here structure emerges: consistent tagging, recurring cost reports per team, and the first steps towards rightsizing and commitment discounts. Teams start to weigh costs in technical decisions, and there is usually a designated owner (or small team) for FinOps within the organisation.

Run: costs as a continuous part of engineering

In the most mature phase cost awareness is woven into the way of working: automated anomaly detection, cost allocation that directly influences teams, and architectural decisions that take cost-effectiveness into account right from the drawing board. Saving is then no longer an action, but a property of the system.

  • Crawl: cost data is visible, but fragmented and reactive
  • Walk: structural reporting, first optimisations, clear ownership
  • Run: automated, proactive, embedded in engineering culture

Important: you don't have to be in the same phase for every practice (cost allocation, optimisation, planning). Most organisations, for example, are far along in visibility but still early in automated optimisation.

Why this classification is practically useful

The model prevents you from reaching for advanced automation too quickly while the basics (consistent tagging, shared ownership, reliable reporting) are still missing. Conversely, it also prevents mature organisations from getting stuck in manual processes that could have been automated long ago.

The first step is simple: map out which phase you are actually in per practice, instead of going on a feeling. That starting point immediately determines which interventions deliver the most.
