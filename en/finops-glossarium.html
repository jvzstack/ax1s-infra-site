FinOps Glossary

Jelle van Zantwijk·31 Jul 2026·6 min read

20 key terms from FinOps and cloud cost management, explained briefly and clearly. No jargon contest, just look up what something means and move on.

AI cost management

Controlling spend on AI and LLM workloads: compute for training and inference, API costs from model providers, and data storage. Because of the rapid growth and variable usage patterns of AI workloads, this is one of the fastest-growing focus areas within FinOps.

Chargeback & Showback

Two ways of making cloud costs visible per team or product. With showback you see the costs but they aren't actually charged; with chargeback the amount is really allocated to the team's budget. Showback is often the first step towards chargeback.

Cloud Financial Management (CFM)

Umbrella term for the financial management of cloud spend, of which FinOps is the most common practice. Besides optimisation, CFM also covers budgeting, forecasting and governance.

Cloud unit economics

The cost of cloud infrastructure expressed per unit of value, for example per customer, per transaction or per API call. This shows whether technology costs grow along with revenue or rise faster: an important signal for CFOs.

Cloud waste

Cloud capacity that is paid for but no longer (or never) used usefully: forgotten test environments, unused storage volumes, oversized instances. Often the first and fastest source of savings in a FinOps engagement. See also our 5 ways to eliminate unnecessary cloud costs.

Commitment-based discount

Collective name for discounts you get in exchange for a usage or spend commitment to a cloud provider, such as Reserved Instances and Savings Plans. The longer and more certain the commitment, the higher the discount generally is. Work it out yourself with our RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator.

Cost allocation

The process of assigning cloud, SaaS and AI costs to the teams, products or cost centres that cause them. Requires consistent tagging and is the foundation for both showback and chargeback.

Cost anomaly detection

Automatically flagging unexpected cost spikes, for example caused by a misconfigured resource or a forgotten process that keeps running. Early detection prevents a mistake from running for weeks before anyone notices.

FinOps

The practice in which finance, engineering and operations work together to reduce waste in cloud, SaaS and AI spend and get more value out of technology. Not a one-off action, but a continuous practice. See the full explanation at What is FinOps?

FinOps Certified Practitioner

A certification from the FinOps Foundation that demonstrates someone understands and can apply the FinOps framework. Both founders of AX1S Infra are FinOps Certified Practitioners.

FinOps Foundation

The non-profit organisation behind the FinOps framework, part of the Linux Foundation. It publishes, among other things, the annual State of FinOps benchmark and manages the FinOps Certified Practitioner certification.

FinOps Lifecycle

The three continuous phases of the FinOps framework: Inform (create visibility), Optimize (save in a targeted way) and Operate (embed optimisation in processes). See What is FinOps? for the full explanation.

Multi-cloud governance

Consistently managing costs, access and policy across multiple cloud providers. Without governance, different tagging standards, security levels and reporting methods quickly emerge per cloud. More on this in Multi-cloud governance without the complexity.

Reserved Instance (RI)

A commitment to a specific instance type in a specific region, generally for 1 or 3 years, in exchange for a substantial discount compared with on-demand rates. Less flexible than a Savings Plan, but often with a slightly higher discount. Compare both options with our RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator.

Rightsizing

Adjusting the capacity of a resource (e.g. a virtual machine) to actual usage. Oversized instances are one of the most common forms of cloud waste.

SaaS-sprawl

The uncontrolled growth of SaaS subscriptions within an organisation, often purchased by individual teams without central oversight. Leads to duplicate tools, forgotten licences and unnecessary costs.

Savings Plan

A commitment to a fixed amount of compute spend per hour, for 1 or 3 years, regardless of instance type or region. More flexible than a Reserved Instance, with a comparable (sometimes slightly lower) discount. Compare both with our RI vs. Savings Plan Calculator or read the background in Reserved Instances vs. Savings Plans.

Spot instance

Unused cloud capacity offered at a heavily reduced rate, with the risk that the capacity can be reclaimed at short notice. Suitable for interruptible workloads such as batch processing.

Tagging (resource tagging)

Labelling cloud resources with metadata such as team, project or environment. The basis for reliable cost allocation: without consistent tagging, accurate reporting per team is almost impossible.

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

The full cost of a technology choice over its entire lifetime, including not only the direct cloud or licence costs but also management, migration and any decommissioning.
