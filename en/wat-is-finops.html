What is FinOps?

Jelle van Zantwijk·31 Jul 2026·5 min read

FinOps brings finance, engineering and operations together to reduce waste in cloud, SaaS and AI spend and to get more value out of technology. It is not a one-off action but a continuous practice in which every team takes ownership of its own costs and usage.

The FinOps Foundation, part of the Linux Foundation, is the non-profit organisation behind this practice. It develops the FinOps framework, the annual State of FinOps benchmark and the FinOps Certified Practitioner certification.

The FinOps Lifecycle: Inform, Optimize, Operate

The FinOps framework describes a continuous cycle of three phases, which is gone through again and again as cloud, SaaS and AI usage changes:

  • Inform: create visibility into who spends what, with correct tagging, allocation and benchmarking, so teams can see their own costs.
  • Optimize: on the basis of that visibility, save in a targeted way: rightsizing, commitments such as Reserved Instances or Savings Plans, and cleaning up unused resources.
  • Operate: embed optimisation in processes and automation, so cost management doesn't stay a one-off project but becomes part of how teams work.

Who is involved in FinOps?

FinOps is cross-functional by definition. Finance brings budget discipline and forecasting, engineering knows the technical options for optimisation, and business/product teams understand which spend creates value. Without these three at the table together, cost management ends up either too technical or too far removed from practice.

How does FinOps differ from traditional cost management?

Traditional cost management is periodic and top-down: an annual budget, checked after the fact by finance. FinOps is continuous and decentralised: teams see their own usage in (near) real time and make decisions themselves, within a shared framework. That fits the nature of cloud, SaaS and AI spend, which can fluctuate per hour or even per API call, unlike the fixed infrastructure costs of the past.

How do you get started with FinOps?

Most organisations start with visibility: knowing who spends what, before optimising. Where an organisation currently stands often differs per practice (cost allocation, optimisation, planning) and doesn't need to be the same everywhere. In the FinOps maturity model you can read how to determine that for your organisation, and 5 ways to eliminate unnecessary cloud costs lists concrete first steps.

A concrete example

An engineering team runs a test environment on heavy instance types because nobody knows exactly which capacity is needed. Without FinOps, finance only sees at the end of the quarter that cloud costs have risen, without knowing why. With FinOps the team sees the usage itself, recognises the overprovisioning within a few weeks and adjusts the instance types based on actual usage. The same logic applies to SaaS subscriptions nobody uses any more and AI workloads whose cost per API call keeps rising: visibility at the team itself is what makes the difference.
